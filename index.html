  • Brennan Baker




    Primary Affiliation

    University of Washington

    Positions Held

    Postdoctoral fellow and NIH Pathway to Independence Award (K99/R00) recipient (UW)

    Research & Interest

    Dr. Baker is an environmental molecular epidemiologist whose research examines how prenatal and early-life exposures influence child neurodevelopment, integrating longitudinal birth cohort studies with genetics and omics approaches. A major focus of his work has been understanding the relationship between prenatal acetaminophen (paracetamol) exposure and child neurodevelopment. His work has used biomarkers of prenatal acetaminophen exposure to investigate associations with child ADHD and potential underlying mechanisms, including alterations in brain connectivity and placental gene expression. Through his K99/R00 award, he is building on this work using data from the Environmental influences on Child Health Outcomes (ECHO) Program and the Copenhagen Prospective Study on Asthma in Childhood (COPSAC) to distinguish potential causal effects from familial confounding and investigate genetic factors that may modify these associations.

    Educational Background & Training

    PhD in Environmental Health Sciences (Columbia University)

  • Giselle Bellia




    Primary Affiliation

    Yale University

    Positions Held

    PhD Candidate (Yale)

    Research & Interest

    Ms. Bellia’s research interests lie at the intersection of environmental epidemiology, pharmacoepidemiology, and causal inference, with a particular focus on understanding how prenatal medication exposures influence child neurodevelopmental outcomes. Her dissertation research examines prenatal acetaminophen exposure and child neurodevelopment using data from the Danish National Birth Cohort. She is also interested in epidemiologic & biostatistical methods and quantitative bias analysis to address potential sources of bias and strengthen causal inference in observational research. Additionally, her work includes simulation studies designed to investigate fundamental biases in epidemiologic and statistical study designs, particularly selection bias and measurement error. Ultimately, her research aims to generate robust, evidence-based findings that advance our understanding of early-life determinants of long-term health and development.

    Educational Background & Training

    MPH in Environmental Health Sciences (Yale University), PhD Candidate in Environmental Health Sciences (Yale University)

  • Eugenia Chock




    Primary Affiliation

    Yale University

    Positions Held

    Assistant Professor of Medicine (Rheumatology, Allergy & Immunology) (Yale)

    Research & Interest

    Dr. Chock is primarily interested in reproductive health, maternal-child health, and pharmacoepidemiology in rheumatic diseases. Dr. Chock leads the PRIME Lab (Pharmacoepidemiology & Real-world data for Immune-mediated disease in Maternal–child Epidemiology), which integrates advanced pharmacoepidemiologic and perinatal epidemiologic methods, machine learning, and real-world patient data to generate clinically actionable evidence. Her research aims to improve early disease detection, optimize medication safety and effectiveness, and inform personalized treatment strategies for women with rheumatic diseases throughout pregnancy, postpartum, and beyond.

  • Andrew "Andy" DeWan




    Primary Affiliation

    Yale University

    Positions Held

    Associate Professor of Epidemiology (Yale), Co-Director of the Center for Perinatal, Pediatric and Environmental Epidemiology (Yale); Director of Graduate Studies, Yale School of Public Health

    Research & Interest

    Dr. DeWan is a genetic epidemiologist with a focus on extending and applying analytical methods to identify genetic susceptibility variants for complex traits. A key theme throughout his work is applying a strategy of delineating narrowly defined phenotypes and stratification by ancestry to reduce heterogeneity and increase statistical power. To better elucidate the genetic architecture of complex traits, his research extends analytical methods to identify genetic interactions as well as pleiotropy. He has applied these genetic mapping methods across a number of diverse phenotypes including asthma, obesity, leukemia, pediatric lung cancer, preeclampsia, preterm birth, and bacterial bloodstream infections. He has been the Principal Investigator of external grants to fund his research (5 NIH grants, including three R01s).

    Educational Background & Training

    BS in Biology (Brandeis University), MPH in Epidemiology (University of Minnesota), PhD in Statistical Genetics (Rockefeller University)

  • Pengfei Guo




    Primary Affiliation

    University of South Carolina

    Positions Held

    Assistant Professor of Epidemiology and Biostatistics (USC)

    Research & Interest

    Dr. Guo is an environmental and perinatal epidemiologist whose research focuses on advancing maternal, child, and women's health across the life course. Her work draws on pregnancy and birth cohorts to investigate the impacts of environmental and pharmaceutical exposures on perinatal outcomes, as well as long-term cardiometabolic and mental health.
