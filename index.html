  • Brennan Baker




    Primary Affiliation

    University of Washington

    Positions Held

    Postdoctoral fellow and NIH Pathway to Independence Award (K99/R00) recipient (UW)

    Research & Interest

    Dr. Baker is an environmental molecular epidemiologist whose research examines how prenatal and early-life exposures influence child neurodevelopment, integrating longitudinal birth cohort studies with genetics and omics approaches. A major focus of his work has been understanding the relationship between prenatal acetaminophen (paracetamol) exposure and child neurodevelopment. His work has used biomarkers of prenatal acetaminophen exposure to investigate associations with child ADHD and potential underlying mechanisms, including alterations in brain connectivity and placental gene expression. Through his K99/R00 award, he is building on this work using data from the Environmental influences on Child Health Outcomes (ECHO) Program and the Copenhagen Prospective Study on Asthma in Childhood (COPSAC) to distinguish potential causal effects from familial confounding and investigate genetic factors that may modify these associations.

    Educational Background & Training

    PhD in Environmental Health Sciences (Columbia University)

  • Giselle Bellia




    Primary Affiliation

    Yale University

    Positions Held

    PhD Candidate (Yale)

    Research & Interests

    Ms. Bellia’s research interests lie at the intersection of environmental epidemiology, pharmacoepidemiology, and causal inference, with a particular focus on understanding how prenatal medication exposures influence child neurodevelopmental outcomes. Her dissertation research examines prenatal acetaminophen exposure and child neurodevelopment using data from the Danish National Birth Cohort. She is also interested in epidemiologic & biostatistical methods and quantitative bias analysis to address potential sources of bias and strengthen causal inference in observational research. Additionally, her work includes simulation studies designed to investigate fundamental biases in epidemiologic and statistical study designs, particularly selection bias and measurement error. Ultimately, her research aims to generate robust, evidence-based findings that advance our understanding of early-life determinants of long-term health and development.

Educational Background & Training

Master of Public Health (Yale University), PhD Candidate (Yale University)
